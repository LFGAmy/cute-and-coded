# cute & coded

**coded, but make it cute.**

a place to learn AI and tech in plain words, for feminine builders. the ones who were told tech isn't for them. it is.

this repo holds the words: every lesson, as a plain markdown file you can read, copy, fix, or send to a friend. the site that teaches them lives at [cuteandcoded.com](https://cuteandcoded.com).

## who it's for

you use ChatGPT sometimes and mostly wing it. you've never opened a terminal, or you have and closed it fast. you like cute things and you are tired of being talked down to. you're smart, you just haven't been shown.

that's who every lesson is written for. no jargon without a definition. no "well actually." nothing dumbed down either: cute enough to save, real enough to use.

## how to get involved

1. **run the terminal.** go to [cuteandcoded.com](https://cuteandcoded.com) and type the three commands it asks for. takes two minutes. nothing you do there can break anything.
2. **add yourself to the builders board.** at the end of the terminal it asks what you're building and where you are. say yes to the board and your card goes up at [cuteandcoded.com/builders](https://cuteandcoded.com/builders) once a person has checked it.
3. **leave one real comment.** pick someone on the board, go to the thing she made, and say something specific. not "love this," a real one. that's the whole move.
4. **come back for the next lesson.** new ones land on the site and in this folder. if you gave an email, it comes to you.

## fix a word, or write a lesson

if a lesson is wrong, unclear, or uses a word it never explained, open an issue or a pull request. that's a real contribution and it gets credited.

if you want to write one, copy any file in `lessons/`, keep the same three sections, and follow the house rules:

- one idea per lesson. if it needs two, it's two lessons.
- every techy word gets explained the first time it appears, in the sentence it appears in.
- the "steal this" part is something the reader can do today, not a summary.
- accurate first, cute second. a wrong lesson that reads well is worse than no lesson.
- plain words. if a sentence would sound strange said out loud to a friend, rewrite it.

lessons that ship on the site are checked by a person for accuracy before they go up.
